We characterize jump dynamics in the aggregate U.S. stock market using roughly 90 years of intraday data. Unscheduled news events, such as wartime developments, drove the bulk of jump activity in the earlier decades of our sample; in recent decades, scheduled news &mdash; especially monetary policy announcements &mdash; accounts for most jump activity. We also show that jump variation helps forecast future excess market returns.
