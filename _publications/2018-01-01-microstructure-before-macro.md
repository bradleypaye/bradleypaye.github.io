We study the predictive power of aggregate stock market illiquidity for both financial and macroeconomic outcomes. Raw illiquidity measures are contaminated by regime shifts (such as decimalization) and by their close relationship to volatility. After adjusting for these effects, aggregate illiquidity predicts future stock market returns and also anticipates future macroeconomic conditions, including output growth and unemployment.
