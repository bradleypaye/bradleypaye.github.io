Aggregate stock return volatility is both persistent and countercyclical. This paper tests whether it is possible to improve volatility forecasts by incorporating information from macroeconomic and financial variables, beyond what is captured by volatility's own history. Several macroeconomic variables carry statistically significant predictive power for future volatility, but this predictive power is concentrated around the onset of recessions.
